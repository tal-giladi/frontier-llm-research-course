# Module 19 glossary terms

- **Goal-driven research** — choosing work by a concrete capability or decision you want to reach and solving the problems in its way; contrasted with idea-driven research (improving on a paper you read). From Schulman's guide. [19.1]
- **Idea-driven research** — choosing work by following the literature and testing an idea to do something better; higher risk of duplicating others. [19.1]
- **Research taste** — the ability to choose problems whose answers matter and can be obtained; trained by making choices explicit and checking them against later evidence. [19.1]
- **Decisive answer** — a result the pre-stated decision rule can act on, including a well-powered null; an underpowered "nothing seen" is not one. [19.1]
- **p_decisive** — the course's estimate of the probability that an experiment gives a decisive answer: power at the smallest effect that matters times the probability that the answer transfers to the decision's scale. [19.1]
- **Proposal score** — value × p_decisive ÷ cost in GPU-hours: expected decision value per GPU-hour, the course's ranking rule for proposals. [19.1]
- **Rank robustness** — the fraction of random perturbations of a proposal set's estimates (each within a factor f) under which the top proposal stays on top. [19.1]
- **Cheap proxy** — a small, fast experiment expected to predict an expensive one; trusted only after reading it along a scale ladder. [19.1]
- **Proxy trend** — the reading of a comparison at 2–3 sizes against the seed noise: below noise, sign flips, grows, shrinks or flat. [19.1]
- **Kill criterion** — the result, written before the runs, that makes you stop a project. [19.1]
- **Nuisance hyperparameter** — a hyperparameter that must be re-optimised for each value of the one under study (often the learning rate) for the comparison to be fair; from the Deep Learning Tuning Playbook. [19.1]
- **Reproducibility (NASEM)** — obtaining consistent computational results with the same data, code and conditions of analysis. [19.2]
- **Replicability (NASEM)** — obtaining consistent results across studies that answer the same question with their own data; a small-scale "reproduction" of a paper with new code and data is closer to this. [19.2]
- **Reproduction tolerance** — the smallest effect that counts as reproducing a claim, stated before the runs and derived from the seed noise. [19.2]
- **Direction vs magnitude** — the two separate decisions of a reproduction: whether the effect has the claimed sign beyond the tolerance, and whether its size matches the published number when that number is on a comparable footing. [19.2]
- **Not comparable (magnitude)** — the reproduction outcome when the published number's metric, scale or setup differs too much to compare sizes. [19.2]
- **Deviation log** — the record of every way a reproduction differs from the paper (scale, data, tokens, optimizer, learning rates, evaluation, seeds, code), with the reason and the expected effect on the claim, and whether it was recorded before or after the results. [19.2]
- **Learning-rate sensitivity** — Wortsman et al.'s summary of a learning-rate sweep: the mean over rates of min(loss, initial loss) minus the best loss of the sweep. [19.2]
- **Claims register** — a fenced YAML block in a research note listing each claim with its runs, changed variables, axis, value, interval, checkpoints, selection split and scope, so it can be checked against run cards. [19.3]
- **Claims linter** — `frontierlab.research.writeup.lint_claims`: checks each registered claim for seeds, uncertainty, budget parity, controls, checkpoint selection and scope. [19.3]
- **Cherry-picked checkpoint** — reporting the best of several evaluations of one arm (or selecting on the test split), which biases the reported number upward. [19.3]
- **Claim beyond evidence** — a sentence that generalises past what the runs measured, for example from a 1.8M-parameter run to frontier models. [19.3]
- **Figure specification** — a small YAML file next to a figure stating its one claim, x-axis, contract axis, uncertainty, seeds per arm and caption, checked by `lint_figure`. [19.3]
- **Written defence** — answers to reviewer questions that give the number from the record and what would change it, instead of adjectives. [19.3]
- **Review template** — the course's structure for reviewing someone else's result: claim restated, claims table, controls and budget, uncertainty, figures, problems with where/evidence/request, recommendation. [19.3]
- **Capstone proposal** — the Module 19 project's experiment contract as data, with what counts as reproduced, one extension, a proxy and a kill criterion; Module 20 starts from it. [19.3]
