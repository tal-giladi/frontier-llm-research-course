- **Gradient weight (of an RL objective)** — the factor multiplying each sampled token's grad log pi in an objective's gradient; RL objectives for LLMs differ only in this weight. [14.1]
- **GRPO** — Group Relative Policy Optimization: PPO's clipped token surrogate averaged per response, then over the group, with group-normalised advantages and a k3 KL term (DeepSeekMath Eq. 3). [14.1]
- **DAPO** — GRPO's surrogate with clip-higher (0.2/0.28), one token-level mean over the group, dynamic sampling (drop all-right and all-wrong groups) and overlong shaping, without KL. [14.1]
- **Dr. GRPO** — GRPO without the 1/|o_i| length normaliser (replaced by a constant) and without dividing advantages by the group's reward std. [14.1]
- **GSPO** — Group Sequence Policy Optimization: one length-normalised sequence ratio s_i (the geometric mean of token ratios), clipped per sequence with ranges of about 3e-4 and 4e-4. [14.1]
- **Sequence ratio (length-normalised)** — s_i = (pi_theta(y_i)/pi_old(y_i))^(1/|y_i|) = exp(mean_t log r_{i,t}). [14.1]
- **GSPO-token** — GSPO variant with s_{i,t} = sg[s_i] pi_theta(y_t)/sg[pi_theta(y_t)]: value s_i, gradient s_i grad log pi(y_t); equals GSPO when advantages are per sequence. [14.1]
- **CISPO** — Clipped IS-weight Policy Optimization (MiniMax-M1): REINFORCE on every token weighted by a capped, stop-gradient importance ratio, token-level mean. [14.1]
- **Stop-gradient (sg)** — a value used in the forward computation but treated as a constant by autograd (`.detach()`). [14.1]
- **Routing Replay** — reusing the old policy's expert choices when computing token ratios of an MoE model, needed by GRPO on MoE in Qwen's runs and unnecessary under GSPO. [14.1]
- **Objective bundle** — an objective together with the settings its paper pairs with it (normaliser, clip range, advantage scale, batch rule); what a team adopts when it switches objectives. [14.2]
- **Tuning budget** — the number of configurations and runs each arm may use to choose its hyperparameters; equal across arms in a fair comparison. [14.2]
- **Control arm (random or format reward)** — an RL arm trained with a reward that carries no task information, scored like every other arm; an RL gain counts only beyond the control's. [14.2]
- **Spurious reward** — a reward unrelated to correctness (random, format-only, incorrect labels) that still raises a model's score, as reported for Qwen2.5-Math. [14.2]
- **Stability metrics** — pre-stated per-run measures of training health: entropy drop, gradient spikes, maximum importance ratio, held-out drawdown. [14.2]
- **Rollout engine** — the inference system (vLLM, SGLang) that generates RL samples, separate from the trainer and holding its own copy of the weights. [14.3]
- **Colocated vs disaggregated rollouts** — engine and trainer on the same GPUs (weights copied in memory, e.g. CUDA IPC) vs on separate GPUs (weights sent over NCCL). [14.3]
- **Staleness (lag)** — how many policy updates separate the weights that generated a batch from the weights being updated with it. [14.3]
- **Bounded staleness** — an asynchronous schedule that never lets a batch's lag exceed k (AReaL's eta). [14.3]
- **One-step off-policy** — staleness bound 1: the next batch is generated while the current update runs. [14.3]
- **In-flight weight update** — loading new weights into the engine while sequences are mid-generation and continuing them (PipelineRL). [14.3]
- **Behaviour policy** — the policy that generated a batch; its log-probabilities belong in the importance ratio's denominator. [14.3]
- **Decoupled PPO** — AReaL's objective separating the behaviour policy (importance weight) from a proximal policy that anchors the trust region. [14.3]
- **Train/inference mismatch** — the difference between the engine's and the trainer's probabilities for the same tokens and weights, from kernels, precision and batching. [14.3]
- **Batch invariance** — a kernel property: a row's result does not depend on the other rows in its batch. [14.3]
- **Weight synchronisation** — copying the trainer's updated weights into the rollout engine after each update (3.4 GB per sync for a 1.7B model in bf16). [14.3]
- **Compute-performance curve** — held-out pass rate against RL compute; ScaleRL fits R_C - R_0 = (A - R_0)/(1 + (C_mid/C)^B). [14.4]
- **Asymptote A** — the pass rate a recipe's sigmoid curve approaches as compute grows. [14.4]
- **Scaling exponent B / midpoint C_mid** — the steepness of a compute-performance sigmoid and the compute at which half of its gain is reached. [14.4]
- **Profile interval** — the set of values of one parameter whose best fit (over the others) is within a chi-square threshold of the overall best; unbounded when the data cannot pin the parameter. [14.4]
- **Identifiability** — whether the data determine a parameter; a short run identifies a combination of A and C_mid, not A itself. [14.4]
- **Coverage** — the fraction of problems a model can solve at all within k samples (pass@k at large k). [14.4]
- **Crossover (pass@k)** — the k at which the base model's pass@k catches up with the RL policy's. [14.4]
- **Sharpening** — RL concentrating probability on solutions the base model could already sample, raising pass@1 more than large-k pass@k. [14.4]
